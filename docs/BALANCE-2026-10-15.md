Spec [SPEC-TOURNAMENT-ARMORY-2026-10-13.md](SPEC-TOURNAMENT-ARMORY-2026-10-13.md) 1B phase 4 (owner-approved): tune the round table, raise the AI card reserve, let the AI reward pick take book moves, and measure the relic snowball. Branch `test-product-2`, Long Board, seed 29000. Rules in [CHESS.md](CHESS.md) "Tournament run" and "Gambits"; linked from [SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md](SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md).

## BLUF

- **Update 2026-10-17:** the AI card reserve is back to 40 and the AI now splits its Tempo by a set share (87% pawns, 13% cards), so it plays about 3.5 cards per Act again; the round table sets the share per court. See "AI Tempo split (2026-10-17)" below.
- **Round table:** opponents are weaker early and much stronger in the Final. Each round now also sets how many relics the opponent may hold (its own cap): Club 0, County 2, Semifinal 4, Final 6. The Final starts with 6 relics and +60 Tempo.
- **Per round (card-playing stand-in, every run plays every round):** Club 60%, County 55%, Semifinal 47%, Final 46%; **18% of runs end Champion**. Before: the later rounds were the easiest (Semifinal 63%, Final 64%).
- **AI card reserve 40 → 50:** card-a-minute player 50.0% → 57.0%, idle player 45.5% → 46.0%, AI vs AI 49.5% White (800). Side effect: the AI now plays about 0.3–1 card per Act (was about 6–7).
- **AI reward pick:** with the relic row full it takes the heirloom or the book move, whichever is worth more gold (book move = 500). Book moves went from 0 picks to about a third of the picks.
- **Relic snowball:** White fills its 5 relics during the County match in about 85% of runs; every Semifinal and Final starts at 5. Recommendation: **keep the cap at 5** (reasons below); owner decides.

## How it was measured

- `npm run tournament -- --runs 100 --seed 29000 --white-player --white-every 60 --workers 2 --ladder`. New tool flags: `--white-every S` (the stand-in plays at most one card per S s, as `npm run balance`), `--no-cards` (the idle stand-in), `--workers N`, `--ladder` (a loss does not end the run: White carries on as if it had won, Spoils included, so all four rounds get 100 matches; the real runs, cut at their first loss, are reported too), and a snowball read (relics at each round's start and end, the Act White hits the cap).
- **Ladder rates are the targets** (the spec's "round ladder": each round against a typical carry). Real-run rates per round are higher, because a run that keeps winning has a strong court for its seed (the court carries), so they overstate how hard later rounds are for an average player.
- Noise: 100 matches per round is about ±10 points; read direction, not decimals.

## AI reward pick

- Before: a relic while the row has room, else the heirloom, else the book move. A piece without an heirloom is almost always on offer, so the AI never took a book move (0 of 125 picks).
- Now: a relic while the row has room; otherwise the heirloom or the book move, whichever is worth more gold: the heirloom is worth its item's price, a book move `tournament.aiBookGold` (500). A tier-1 heirloom (250–300 gold) loses to a book move; a tier-2 one (860–900) beats it. Picks in the shipped-table runs: relic 65, heirloom 23, book 34. The browser's **Let the AI choose** uses the same rule (glossary "Spoils" updated).

- White drafts about 2.4 relics in the Club match (one per Act break), takes one more as Spoils, and fills the row in the second Act of County. From then on every relic draft is a swap.
- **Recommendation: keep the cap at 5.** The snowball was the reason the late rounds were easy before; Black's own cap per round now carries that difficulty without taking anything from the player (the Final is 46% with White at 5 relics). A cap of 4 would not stop the row filling early: White would reach 4 at County's first Act break instead, so the "growth stops in round 2" feeling would come one Act sooner. If the owner wants growth to last to the Final, the lever is the number of in-match drafts in a run (for example one draft per match), not the cap. A cap of 4 would also need the table retuned (the late rounds would get harder for White).

## AI Tempo split (2026-10-17)

Owner-approved root-cause fix for the open choice above: the AI's cards and its pawn plan no longer compete through two reserves. Long Board, seed 29000, 200 matches per probe unless noted (about ±7 points of noise).

- **Rule:** an AI side spends 87% of its Tempo on pawns and 13% on cards over the match (`pawns.aiPawnShare` 0.87); whichever is behind its share buys next and the other waits, unless the one behind has nothing to buy (every lane full or the pawn cap reached; no card in hand that fits). The pawn plan's reserve (40) and the card reserve (`pawns.aiCardReserve`, back to **40**) still apply. White's plan is unchanged.
- **Why a share:** with two reserves, whichever fires at the lower Tempo takes nearly everything (reserve 50: 0.3–1 card per Act; reserve 40: 6–7). A share sets the split directly and does not move when card costs or Tempo income change.
- **Tournament:** a round can set its court's own share (`rounds[].aiPawnShare`): the Club court plays few cards (0.97), the Final court more (0.80).
