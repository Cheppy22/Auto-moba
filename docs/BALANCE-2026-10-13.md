# Tempo tune (2026-10-13)

Item 4.0 of [FABLE-REVIEW-1-2026-10-11.md](FABLE-REVIEW-1-2026-10-11.md): the player should be able to play a card inside the first 60 s of Act 1 and have a real card choice about every 60–90 s. Numbers only. Branch `test-product-2`, Long Board. Linked from [SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md](SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md); rules in [CHESS.md](CHESS.md) "Pawnlings and pawns" and "Gambits".

## BLUF

- **One number changed:** White's standing pawn plan keeps back **40 Tempo** before it fields a pawn (was 20; now `pawns.planReserve` in `content/tuning.json`). Start Tempo (30), income (+1/s), card costs (10–45) and the AI are unchanged.
- **Why that one:** the measurements showed the first card was never the problem (a card is playable from 0 s in every match). The plan was: it spent every point above 35 on pawns, so Tempo sat between 20 and 35 and cards costing 25–45 were out of reach most of Act 1, however long the player waited.
- **Effect for a player who leaves auto pawns on** (100 matches each): Act 1 time with all three cards playable 16% → 74%; with at most one playable 48% → 9%; cards that left the hand without ever being playable 18% → 0.4%; Tempo held in Act 1 about 24 → 42. The plan still fields pawns (about 54 → 48 per match).
- **AI-vs-AI balance is unchanged by construction** (the AI never uses the plan; `npm run baseline` is identical on both maps). The 800-match runs below are this build's numbers with the new pacing metrics: White 49.2% / 51.8% (seeds 29000 / 31000), median 14.8 min, p90 22.3 / 22.6 min, 25.4 / 25.1 kills per match, identical to phase 4.

## Method

- New read-outs in the balance tool (`tools/simulate.ts`, `tools/report.ts`, section "Tempo pacing" of every report), read every tick: first second a card in the hand is playable, first card played, cards played per Act, seconds between card plays, Act 1 time with 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 playable cards, Act 1 cards that left the hand (played or expired) without ever being playable, mean Tempo in Act 1, and the eye badge (below).
- **Player runs** (`--white-player`): White plays as the browser sets up a player: the standing pawn plan 2 / 2 / 2 fields its pawns. `--white-every 60` adds a stand-in that plays the AI's best-fitting card at most once per 60 s (a person who checks the hand now and then); with `--no-gambits-A` White plays no cards. Black is the usual AI. Tuning probes used `BALANCE_TUNING` overrides, 100 matches, seed 29000.
- Full runs: `npm run balance -- --map long --matches 800 --seed S --quiet --workers 2`, seeds 29000 and 31000, one job at a time.

## The high-ground eye badge (measurement only)

Read every tick on every living piece, 800 matches on each seed (both seeds and the player probes agree to within 0.1):

- **On for 2.3% of living piece-time.** About 20 s per piece in a 15-minute match.
- **It turns on often but briefly:** 17.2 times per piece per match, about 1.2 per piece per minute; across the ten pieces about 11 showings a minute somewhere on the board. The mean showing lasts **1.1 s**; only 5.6 showings per piece per match (a third) last 1 s or more.
- **Verdict:** not rare, just short. It lights when a piece's current target stands a tier below, and targets change or step onto a ramp within a second, so the badge mostly blinks. That fits both pass-2 playtests missing it.

## Not changed

- Start Tempo 30, +1 per second, kill and Bastion Tempo, every card cost, the AI's own reserve (the cost of the card it wants, else 20), Throne, respawn and kills.
- The square map (`?map=square`): the plan reserve is global, so a square-map player gets the same change; its AI-vs-AI baseline is identical, so no square balance run was needed.

## Open choices

- The standing pawn plan makes a hands-off player win about 80% against the AI. Options: lower the default plan (2 / 2 / 2 → 1 / 1 / 1), make the Black AI field more pawns, or accept it until difficulty tiers or named opponents (3A / 3B).
- The eye badge shows often but briefly (see above); options for the art pass: hold it on for a minimum time (for example 2 s) so it can be seen, or cover it in the glossary and a first-time nudge (onboarding, 4.5).
